Reader

      
      

        Controls whether the reader offers to load a previously cached
        or relay-published copy of the page. "Always" surfaces any
        non-identical archive; "Only when richer" preserves the older
        paywall-shaped heuristic.
      


      
      

        A standalone page for following other researchers by npub and
        reading what they publish — articles, claims, assessments,
        verdicts, case briefs. Follows are stored locally and never
        published; the feed is pull-only. Off by default while the
        phase is in flight.
      


      
      

        Enables the Network page's "Mirror follow list" action: your
        global follows publish as a standard NIP-02 contact list under
        your primary identity — public, and irrevocable in practice.
        Case- and entity-scoped follows never publish. Every publish
        merges with your existing remote list first (never a blind
        replace), so a contact list you maintain in another client
        survives. Off by default.
      


      

      
Cases

      

        A case is an isolated project workspace owned by a profile — its
        own entities, claims, archive, audits, and signed-event journal.
        New case creates and binds everything in one
        step and switches you into it; every capture then joins the case
        automatically. Switching cases moves the storage namespace
        and the signing identity together — reload any
        open X-Ray tabs after switching.
      

      

      

        
        
        
        
      

      

        Deleting a case workspace requires typing its name and downloads
        its backup first. The default workspace cannot be deleted.
      

      

      

      
Active case data

      

        These act on the active case's workspace —
        everything you have captured or authored in it: entities (and
        their keypairs, including the entity-sync key), claims,
        evidence links, assessments, forensic findings, truth
        adjudications, platform accounts, the archive cache, audit
        records, and the signed-event journal. Settings, relays,
        feature flags, the LLM key, your saved
        identities, and other cases are untouched.
      

      

        
        
      

      

        The backup contains your saved identities and their private
        keys — treat the file like an nsec. Audit records cost money to
        recompute; the reset flow offers the backup first.
      

      

      

      
Full backup

      

        Everything the extension stores locally in one file: the workspace
        above plus settings, saved identities, the captured
        article archive, original source documents, audit records, and the
        signed-event journal. Restoring it on a fresh install brings the
        whole extension back. It contains private keys — treat the file
        like an nsec. Your LLM API key is never included.
      

      
      

        
        
        
        
      

      

        Restore replaces everything with the backup's
        contents — a safety backup of the current data downloads first.
        Import & merge instead accrues a backup's
        content into the current corpus: articles, claims, entities,
        analysis records, and audits it has and you don't are added
        (deduplicated by id; per-article extraction records merge at the
        assertion level), nothing local is deleted or overwritten, and
        settings/identities in the file are ignored.
        The signed-events bundle is the raw signed JSON of every event you
        have published (no keys inside) — anyone can rebroadcast it to any
        relay, so your corpus survives a relay outage.
      

      
      
      
      
      


      

      
      
Assessments & claim links


      
      

        Off by default. When on, the reader's Publish batch also emits your
        judgments — kind-30054 assessments (stance + labels + rationale),
        kind-30055 claim links, and a one-time kind-1985 label mirror per
        labeled assessment — signed by your publishing identity and
        publicly visible to anyone with relay access.
        Local capture always works regardless of this switch.
      


      

      
Epistemic audits

      
      

        Off by default. When on, the reader's Publish batch also emits
        the published article's imported audit results (kinds
        30056/30057), its prediction ledger entries (30058), and your
        resolutions of its predictions (30059) — scoped to that one
        article per publish, signed by your publishing identity, and
        publicly visible to anyone with relay access.
        A published low score is a public, attributed, disputable
        record. Local import and rendering never need this switch.
      

      

        Audits normally run inside X-Ray — open a capture in the reader
        and use Quick or Thorough; the
        result is stored for you and needs nothing here. Import is for
        an audit produced elsewhere, such as by the
        docs/auditor-prototype/scorer/ research CLI.
        Imports are re-hashed and schema-validated and must match a
        local capture — the audit has to be about text you actually
        captured. Importing stores results locally only; publishing
        audit events is a separate, flag-gated step.
      

      

        
        
        
      

      

      

      
Forensic findings

      
      

        Off by default. When on, the reader's Publish batch also emits your
        behavioral findings — kind-30062 (the named maneuver + its evidence
        chain + a required counter-read), a one-time kind-1985 maneuver
        mirror, and any revision/* story-change edges
        (kind-30055) — signed by your publishing identity and
        publicly visible to anyone with relay access. A
        finding names a structure, not a verdict; it labels the
        subject's pubkey, so it only publishes for a subject you've tagged
        as a keyed entity. Local capture always works regardless.
      


      

      
Truth adjudication

      
      

        Off by default. When on, the reader's Publish batch also emits your
        adjudicated verdicts — kind-30063 (a descriptive ruling on a
        proposition, with its declared standard of proof, verbatim evidence,
        and required caveats) plus a one-time kind-1985 mirror labeling the
        claim — and your integrity findings (kind-30064, a subject's stated
        words vs enacted deeds), signed by your publishing identity and
        publicly visible to anyone with relay access. A
        verdict attaches to a proposition, never a person; an
        integrity finding labels the subject's pubkey, so it only publishes
        for a subject tagged as a keyed entity, and only once every claim
        it references is itself published. Local adjudication (the reader's
        🏛 flow) always works regardless.
      


      

      
Entity corpus

      
      

        Off by default. When on, the reader's Publish batch emits each
        tagged entity's kind-0 profile (an honestly-labeled research
        record: maintainer line, type, aliases) and one kind-1
        mention note per tagged entity ("Mentioned in
        <article>", with the verbatim mention quote) so following an
        entity's npub in any NOSTR client surfaces its corpus — capped at
        10 per article, never re-sent for the same article version.
        These are signed by the entity's own keys and publicly
        visible to anyone with relay access; once relayed they are
        irrevocable in practice — NIP-09 deletion requests are
        best-effort only and many relays ignore them. Re-publishes happen
        automatically only when a profile's content actually changed.
        Local dossiers always work regardless.
      


      

      
Identity sharing

      
      

        Off by default. When on, the reader's Publish batch also emits a
        kind-32126 record for each platform account this capture touched
        (post author, commenters) and each account linked to a tagged
        entity — the cross-user rendezvous: anyone can then discover your
        capture of an account and which entity you say it belongs
        to. Your captured-account → entity links become
        public. For adversarial casework, prefer per-case
        identities (Signing tab).
      


      

      
LLM assist

      
      

        Off by default. When on and an API key is set, the
        reader gains a ✨ Suggest… control that asks Anthropic's
        Claude to propose capture artifacts (choose which types below) from the
        open article. The article text is sent to Anthropic
        when you run a pass. Every suggestion is a draft you review and
        confirm — nothing is saved or published automatically, and
        publishing still requires the experimental publish switches above.
      

      
      

        Stored locally on this device, never published, never included in any
        export, and never logged. 
      

      
      

        Suggest these artifact types
        

      

      

        Default: Entities + Claims — extraction
        the model does reliably (high signal, low noise). Relationships,
        assessments, and forensic findings are opt-in judgments:
        higher false-positive rate, and X-Ray never renders them automatically.
      

      

        
        
      


      

      

      
AI vision

      
      

        Off by default, and independent of LLM assist above. When on
        and an Anthropic API key is set (same key field as
        LLM assist), the reader gains a Describe images…
        control: pick which of the capture's images to send, and the model
        returns a factual caption for each — plus a verbatim transcription
        when an image contains legible text (a scanned page, a chart, a
        document photo). The selected images are sent to
        Anthropic — along with the article's title and URL and each
        image's alt text and caption (context that improves the
        naming rules) when you run a pass — one API call per image,
        never automatically. Every caption and transcription is a
        draft you accept per image; accepted notes merge
        into the article body labeled with the model that wrote them, so
        the AI provenance stays visible in the published text.
      


      

      
Capture automation

      
      

        Off by default. When on, opening any page whose address ends in
        #xray:capture captures it and opens the reader —
        exactly as the toolbar button would. This exists so a driving
        agent (the xray-capture skill) can queue captures by
        navigation alone; the marker is stripped before capture and never
        reaches the stored URL. It captures pages, nothing more: no
        accepting, no publishing. Leave off unless an agent session is
        driving.

Case synthesis

      
      

        Off by default. Requires LLM assist and an Anthropic
        API key (same key field above). When on, a case's portal dashboard
        gains an Analyze corpus button that reads every member
        article — a summary, the positions present, the cruxes of disagreement,
        the load-bearing claims, and reviewable cross-article proposals.
        A run sends every member article's text to Anthropic
        (one call per article, then one synthesis call), so it costs
        proportionally more than a single-article pass. The brief is stored
        locally and exportable; it carries no verdict or score. Proposals only
        materialize as ordinary claims/links after you Accept them.
      


      
      

        Off by default. Requires case synthesis (above) plus LLM assist and
        the API key. When on, every capture that lands in a case-bound
        workspace immediately runs the synthesis map stage for that
        one article, so a later Analyze corpus starts from a
        warm cache instead of re-reading the whole corpus.
        This is a standing authorization to spend: each capture sends
        that article’s text to Anthropic (one map call per capture,
        roughly the cost of one Suggest pass) — with it off, the
        same work happens only when you click Analyze or Pre-analyze and
        confirm. Already-analyzed articles are skipped (cache first, no
        call), and a failure never affects the capture itself.
